# Credits

**Inside the Impact** by Joohyun Lee. A boxing safety-education game for a college extracurricular.

This is a fictional educational game, not a medical device, examination, diagnosis, treatment or clearance tool. It has not been reviewed by a qualified sports-medicine or concussion clinician, and no professional endorsement is claimed. Have any suspected injury assessed by qualified clinicians. Emergency in South Korea: call 119.

## Medical knowledge

The game's Medical Codex holds 19 entries, which unlock as you meet them in a bout:

- Possible concussion
- Serious brain injury / emergency warning signs
- Possible liver or spleen injury
- Cuts and facial injuries
- Eye injuries
- Hand fractures and hand injuries
- What actually injures boxers
- Weight cutting and dehydration
- Return to sport after concussion
- Pre-fight screening and ringside observations
- Warm-up and injury prevention
- Training load and overtraining
- Sleep and recovery
- Hydration and fuelling
- Repeated head impacts
- Why you can’t “play through” it
- Protective gear: wraps, gloves, mouthguard
- Rib and chest injuries
- First response to a knockout

### Sources

The game's About panel lists these, with the date they were checked:

- CDC HEADS UP: Signs and Symptoms of Concussion (updated 2025-09-15)
- CDC HEADS UP: Returning to Sports (updated 2025-09-15)
- American Academy of Neurology: Sports Concussion Position Statement
- Neidecker et al., Association of Ringside Physicians: Concussion management in combat sports, consensus statement (Br J Sports Med. 2019;53:328–333. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2017-098799)
- Patricios et al., Consensus statement on concussion in sport: 6th International Conference, Amsterdam 2022 (Br J Sports Med. 2023;57:695–711. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2023-106898)
- Soligard et al., IOC consensus statement on load in sport and risk of injury (Br J Sports Med. 2016;50:1030–1041)
- Walsh et al., Sleep and the athlete: narrative review and 2021 expert consensus recommendations (Br J Sports Med. 2021;55(7))
- McKee et al., CTE: criteria for neuropathological diagnosis and relationship to repetitive head impacts (Acta Neuropathol. 2023)
- Bledsoe et al., Incidence of injury in professional boxing (South Med J. 2005) — source for the injury base rates (facial laceration ~51%, hand ~17%, eye ~14%)
- Bernick et al., Longitudinal change in regional brain volumes in professional fighters (Neurology 2020) — thalamic volume decline, and the finding that bout exposure rather than sparring volume carried the signal
- McCrory & Berkovic, Second impact syndrome (Neurology 1998;50:677–683) — the review finding none of 17 published cases definite
- Echemendia et al., Sport Concussion Assessment Tool 6 (SCAT6) (Br J Sports Med. 2023;57:622–631)
- American Dental Association: Athletic mouth protectors (mouthguards)
- The Case for Protective Headguards in Amateur Boxing and Implications for International Policy on Headguard Bans
- Franchini et al., Weight loss in combat sports: physiological, psychological and performance effects (J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2012;9:52)

The CDC signs/symptoms and returning-to-sports pages were checked on 2026-10-25. The other references are retained from the original, not newly verified. Before public or professional use, request qualified clinician review.
